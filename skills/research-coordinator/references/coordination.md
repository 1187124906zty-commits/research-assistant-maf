# Task context, contribution and decisions

## Generalist leadership

The leader must understand hypotheses, controls, uncertainty and scope well enough to ask useful questions. It does not need to reproduce every expert derivation. Ask specialists to explain which technical uncertainty affects which decision and why. If the basis exceeds the leader's competence, request a bounded expert explanation or independent review rather than accepting an unexplained PASS.

The leader chooses allocation and direction. Specialists can challenge a faulty assignment and propose a better question; they cannot silently expand it into whole-project optimization. Reviewers identify affected claims and remedies, without acquiring an unlimited project veto. The leader also needs to justify repeated extensions; it cannot escape a failed rationale by granting a larger budget.

## Assignment packet

A useful packet includes:

- project purpose, current question and stage;
- the bounded question, why its answer matters and pending decision;
- relevant claims, located facts, hypotheses and material counterevidence;
- exact inputs and versions, available tools and assumptions;
- assigned output paths and changes outside ownership;
- adequacy/acceptance rationale, budget and return condition.

Use an explanation rather than a numeric tolerance when adequacy requires expert assessment. The expert may propose a justified criterion before a consequential comparison. Changing a criterion after viewing results to rescue a failure is unacceptable.

Workers receive sufficient context to act independently and may read linked originals or ask a precise missing-context question. Do not send unrelated history or compress away the condition that limits a conclusion. For independent first-pass review, provide the question and raw evidence before the producer's interpretation when possible.

## Contribution-bearing return

Return a compact explanation alongside actual artifacts:

1. **Answer:** what is now known about the assigned question, including an inconclusive outcome.
2. **Evidence:** observations, source/run paths, conditions, versions and checks; separate observations from inference.
3. **Contribution:** uncertainty reduced, explanations retained/eliminated, tool feasibility learned and claim or argument consequences.
4. **Limits:** specialist adequacy rationale, failed checks, contrary evidence and unresolved assumptions.
5. **Cost and next option:** effort spent and the least costly informative follow-up, or why no follow-up is warranted now.

New files, more digits or fluent text are not progress by themselves. A credible negative result can save substantial work. Receiving a result file and supporting its hypothesis are separate decisions.

The leader records its own disposition: accepted/partial delivery, consequences for understanding, affected claims, surviving ambiguity and next action. Important evidence use requires inspection of the original artifacts. A worker's self-evaluation is an input to this interpretation, not its replacement.

## Shared understanding and recovery

Keep compact project understanding, per-task context and raw artifacts separate. Shared understanding carries current facts, live hypotheses, rejected explanations, resource conditions, material uncertainties and next decisions. Preserve why a research direction changed. Detailed extracts, code, models and logs remain in located artifacts.

Merge changed understanding after returns; do not merge entire conversations. Regenerate task contexts against current state when reassigning or resuming. Check pending dispositions, invalidated claims and source versions first. Reuse unchanged evidence instead of rerunning it because another role is now active.

## Disagreement and proportional effort

Record the disputed statement, relevant evidence, conditions, affected claims and smallest distinguishing test. Separate a factual failure from an optional improvement or stylistic preference. Wrong units, nonconservative physics, data leakage and fabrication cannot be cleared by consensus or stronger wording.

Scope reduction retires the original stronger claim while retaining its history. A valid negative test may be accepted as work while contradicting the hypothesis. A failed comparison does not invalidate unrelated evidence. Preserve unresolved disagreements after choosing a route.

Use short dispositions for routine reversible work. Use explicit handoffs and independent review for expensive runs, important mechanism claims and final manuscript use. More discussion, roles and records do not establish higher quality.
