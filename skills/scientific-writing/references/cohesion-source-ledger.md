## DUKE_SUBJECTS_ACTIONS

[Lesson 1: Subjects and Actions](https://sites.duke.edu/scientificwriting/lesson-1-subjects-and-actions/)

Authority: Duke Graduate School Scientific Writing Resource, Nathan Sheffield/Graduate School institutional lesson.

Actual read scope: Complete substantive lesson: introduction; Principles 1–3 and revision techniques; Examples 1–4; About This Guide footer. Linked lesson1.pdf worksheet not read.

- Principles: Use the main verb for the intended scientific action. Short quotation: "Put actions in verbs"
- Principle 2 / Revision Technique: Inspect subject continuity and intentional topic shifts. Short quotation: "Highlight the subject of each sentence."
- Principle 1: A nominalization can supply a useful backward link and is not inherently defective. Short quotation: "There is nothing inherently wrong with nominalizations"
- Principle 3 / Revision Technique: Investigate subject–verb separation where the reader must reconstruct the main action. Short quotation: "Identify the main subject and its verb in your sentence."

## DUKE_COHESION_COHERENCE

[Lesson 2: Cohesion, Coherence, and Emphasis](https://sites.duke.edu/scientificwriting/lesson-2-cohesion-coherence-and-emphasis/)

Authority: Duke Graduate School institutional writing lesson.

Actual read scope: Complete substantive lesson: definitions; Principles 1–3 and revision techniques; all displayed examples; About This Guide footer. Linked worksheet, passive-voice focus articles and dangling-modifier focus article not read.

- Principle 1: A recognizable premise can introduce new information and reduce backward reconstruction. Short quotation: "begin sentences with familiar (old) information"
- Principle 1 / Revision Technique: Use a temporary old/new-information diagnosis on actual prose. Short quotation: "underline any pieces of new information"
- Principle 2 / Ambiguous characters: A citation can leave execution attribution versus method provenance unclear; name the actor where necessary. Short quotation: "who sequenced the DNA?"
- Principle 2 / Advantage of passive voice: Active/passive selection can serve information order; no voice alone confers objectivity. Short quotation: "Choose passive voice for a reason"
- Principle 3 / Revision Technique: Cohesion between sentences and coherence around a paragraph point are separate checks. Short quotation: "make sure each sentence in a paragraph supports the main point"

Source license: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Credit Duke University Graduate School and link to its resource; copied/adapted licensed material retains the noncommercial and share-alike terms. This output uses short attribution and independently composed methods, not a copied lesson collection.

- Transfer boundary: Old/new order is a reader expectation, not a mandatory order for every sentence.
- Transfer boundary: First/last topic agreement is a diagnostic, not required identical wording.
- Transfer boundary: Nature/Science quotations on the lesson are not current journal-author instructions checked here.

## PURDUE_REVERSE_OUTLINE

Authority: The Writing Lab and Purdue OWL; institution-hosted instructional exercise. Page footer says materials collect contributor ideas and do not express Purdue University positions.

Actual read scope: Complete substantive two-step exercise, revision explanation and legal notices. Linked fair-use terms not read.

- Two-step process / Step 1: Identify the actual paragraph topic. Short quotation: "write down the topic of each paragraph"
- Two-step process / Step 2: Assess each paragraph's role and arrangement in the complete argument. Short quotation: "how the paragraph topic advances the overall argument"

Source license: Copyright; no open license observed. Only minimal quotation, link and independently composed method are shared; full extracted guide is a private reading record. No redistribution permission is claimed.

- Transfer boundary: Its 5–10-word tactic is not an acceptance threshold for a scientific paragraph.

## UNC_SCIENTIFIC_STYLE_R5

[Scientific Writing](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/sciences/)

Authority: University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill Writing Center institutional handout.

Actual read scope: Complete substantive handout: genre/purpose; precision; level of detail; quantification; language/sentence structure; verbosity; objectivity/passive voice; limits; works consulted/footer. Works-consulted books not read.

- Word and phrasing choice: Keep precise scientific terms rather than decorative synonyms. Short quotation: "repetition is preferable to ambiguity"
- Sentence structure: Clear main subject/action can reduce the need to reread. Short quotation: "The subject and the action are now close together"
- Acknowledging your limitations: Scientific scope must remain evidence-based. Short quotation: "Your conclusions should be directly supported by the data"

- Transfer boundary: Handout sentence/preposition advice is heuristic; no automatic word/distance thresholds.
- Transfer boundary: The active-voice trend claim is not a current survey or current author-guideline verification.
