# Synthesize sources and support assertions

For a consequential sentence, ask exactly what it claims and whether the cited passage supports that specificity, direction and conditions. Distinguish direct source statements, a synthesis of multiple sources and your inference. A paper introducing a broad transport process does not necessarily justify a factor-specific mechanism. Use full text for pivotal methods and findings when available; label abstract-only coverage.

Build paragraphs around a question or relation, not a sequence of author summaries. Compare assumptions, methods, conditions and findings. Explain disagreements and what they imply for the current work. Cite a primary source for an original finding where possible and a review for a broad synthesis when that is its actual role.

Verify bibliographic metadata and locators. Reuse a reference if it genuinely supports a second use; do not add citations to increase apparent authority. Preserve contrary findings and avoid claiming exhaustive novelty from a limited search. Quotations, paraphrases and adapted ideas need appropriate attribution. Cite the original provenance of reused equations or methods, and label a local derivation or proposed extension.

For repairs, return a table or compact ledger of affected sentence, cited/source passage, support judgment and action: supported unchanged, rephrased within support, replaced/added source, explicitly inferred or removed. Do not imply that every sentence requires a citation; general transitions and the author's own evidence-bound interpretation have different roles.

Read [source-ledger.md](source-ledger.md) for the institution sources behind the writing guidance, and inspect originals when attribution is at issue. Project scientific citations remain separate from these writing-method sources.
