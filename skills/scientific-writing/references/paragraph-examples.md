# Connected paragraphs: illustrative teaching examples

All scientific content below is invented for teaching. It is not a source quotation, literature fact, result of an executed study or manuscript text to adopt. Transfer the reasoning and information order only; use the current study's actual objects, conditions and evidence. These examples show one possible style, not compulsory structures. For original source-backed passages use [library-use.md](library-use.md#selection-contract) and verified locators.

<a id="abstract-example"></a>

## Abstract: make the question and answer recoverable

**Illustrative paragraph.** Predicting solute transport through fractured rock requires knowing whether the fracture controls the whole sample or only the earliest arrival. We compared tracer transport in matched fractured and intact specimens under the same imposed flow. The fracture advanced the first arrival, but it did not produce a corresponding change in the later concentration decay. This difference suggests that rapid transport through the fracture and slow exchange with the surrounding matrix affect different parts of the response. The comparison therefore identifies where a single effective transport coefficient would lose information, rather than establishing one coefficient for all observation times.

The object is recognizable before method labels appear. The comparison and contrasting observations carry the answer; the implication follows from them with a boundary. A real abstract would replace the invented observations with located findings and give quantitative anchors when needed to assess their magnitude.

<a id="introduction-example"></a>

## Introduction: previous achievement earns the next question

**Illustrative adjacent paragraphs.** Effective diffusion models offer a practical way to predict transport without resolving every pore. Their success in describing the average response makes them useful for comparing specimens whose internal geometry is unavailable. That agreement, however, may leave different transport paths indistinguishable: a model fitted to the average response can conceal whether a small connected region carries the earliest flux.

This distinction matters when the first arrival, rather than the long-time average, determines exposure. Here we compare connected pore networks with the same porosity but different transport-path arrangements and examine both arrival times and the subsequent response decay. Holding porosity common separates the path comparison from a change in total pore volume, while the two response measures test whether the difference persists throughout transport. The study is designed to establish when an effective description retains the information needed for the exposure question.

The second paragraph uses the first paragraph's unresolved distinction. The objective and route follow without a list of contributions or a claim that the proposed comparison succeeded. In a real Introduction, predecessor achievement and limitation require original sources; this invented example does not establish either.

## Methods: explain the role before the procedure

**Illustrative paragraph.** To examine how transport-path arrangement affects the outlet response, we constructed two connected pore networks with the same total pore volume and external dimensions. One contains a direct inlet-to-outlet path with side branches; the other connects the inlet and outlet through winding paths. We calculate transient solute diffusion from an initially solute-free state, using the same diffusivity, inlet concentration pulse and zero outlet concentration in both networks. The outlet solute flux then provides a common response to the imposed pulse. This comparison changes the path arrangement while retaining porosity and material diffusivity. It tests the consequence of the two specified geometries; it does not isolate topology from every associated change in path length or local pore shape.

The design need explains why objects and retained factors enter. The model is introduced through its use before a short referent can depend on it. The final condition is included because it changes what the comparison can answer, not to append every imaginable limitation.

## Results and interpretation: an adverse outcome changes understanding

**Illustrative paragraph.** The direct-path network produced an earlier outlet response than the winding-path network, as shown by the matched outlet flux histories in Fig. 2a. Their later decay rates were similar, despite the difference in first arrival. Thus, the two path arrangements differed in rapid transport without producing the same distinction in the later response. The spatial concentration maps in Fig. 2b help explain this separation: transport first follows the through-paths, whereas later concentration changes extend into the side branches. These observations are consistent with exchange between fast and slow regions, although they do not distinguish exchange from every alternative source of a long tail. A comparison of concentration within the slow regions would address that remaining explanation more directly.

The display sequence supports a decision: matched histories establish the contrast, spatial maps bear on its interpretation, and a discriminating follow-up names the unresolved alternative. Figure numbers and observations are invented. A real paragraph requires actual displays and would retain contrary cases, uncertainty and any checks affecting this inference.
