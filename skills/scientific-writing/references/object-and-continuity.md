# Scientific objects and sentence continuity

This editorial method adapts Duke Lessons 1–2 and UNC paragraph/transition teaching; provenance and limits are in [cohesion-source-ledger.md](cohesion-source-ledger.md). It is not a fixed grammatical pattern.

Recover a difficult passage's scientific core: actual entity, action/relation, affected quantity and condition/comparator. Check the originating source or result. Calculations, measurements, fitted values, local definitions and interpretations have different roles even when numbers coincide. Define comparisons by what varies and what remains common before attaching short codes.

Introduce an unfamiliar entity through why it enters this question. At first substantive model use explain whether the study adopts, formulates or modifies it, identify the formulation and consequential assumptions, and state its role. Attribute borrowed formulations and show local changes. Then reuse a short stable name or symbol; restore a qualifier when a changed comparison needs it. A long compound name cannot supply this relation or establish novelty.

Read a difficult sentence with its predecessor. Begin with a recognizable premise when useful and give the new distinction appropriate emphasis. Resolve pronouns and modifier attachment. Temporarily trace subject, action, prior premise and new information if necessary, then remove the markup. Familiar-to-new ordering helps when it clarifies the relation; it is not mandatory in every sentence.

Read a difficult paragraph with its neighbors. Identify its controlling point, evidence/reasoning and what it leaves the next paragraph able to say. Distinguish local cohesion from coherence of the whole argument. A repeated noun or connector cannot repair an absent inference: move information or supply the premise. For inherited work recover predecessor achievement, specific unresolved aspect, present change and assessment. Distinguish the author's stated limitation from one inferred here.

Define terms where needed, with brief recall for a self-contained abstract or changed local condition. Prefer established terminology; introduce an acronym for a distinct object worth reusing. Judge the repair by whether the intended reader can explain its objects and relations, not by connector counts.
