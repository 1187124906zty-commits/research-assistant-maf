# Argument and intended readers

Begin with the reader's question and the contribution this paper can defend. Keep a provisional chain from problem, relevant prior knowledge and unresolved issue to approach, evidence and bounded answer. Update this chain when evidence changes. Do not force a novelty claim beyond the actual comparison, explanation, replication or applicability result.

Paper architecture follows the work. Empirical work usually needs methods, results and interpretation. A methods/system-design paper without evaluation explains the motivating problem, requirements, method, interfaces, rationale, boundaries and implications; it cannot claim measured effectiveness. A theory paper carries assumptions, derivations and consequences. A review explains its synthesis basis and competing interpretations. Journal-specific requirements override generic ordering where applicable.

Each section performs one main job. Its opening should supply what the reader needs to enter that job, sometimes linking a prior finding to the next question. Headings should name the subject or decision where useful. Paragraphs develop a point with support and interpretation; avoid a heading followed by unrelated lists, repeated claims of importance and summaries that do not advance the argument.

Reorder the text when definitions or assumptions arrive too late. Keep necessary scientific justification in the main paper. Supplemental methods and reproduction files can carry routine implementation and provenance but cannot hide a premise needed to assess a conclusion. Concision means removing duplication and low-value detail, not removing uncertainty, definitions or necessary source support.

Official journal instructions, observed conventions in comparable papers and local editorial preferences have different status. Verify current instructions when claiming compliance. Use comparable articles to learn information order and explanation depth without copying sentences or treating a small sample as universal rules.
