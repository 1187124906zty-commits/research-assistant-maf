# Chapter responsibilities and evidence-dependent writing

This is the project's coordination method, synthesized from MIT_INTRODUCTION, MIT_ABSTRACT, MIT_METHODS, PLOS_STRUCTURE and UNC_PARAGRAPHS in [source-ledger.md](source-ledger.md). It is not a journal rule or a requirement for a particular team size. For a major revision use it before separate chapter assignments; for a small edit use only the affected dependency.

## One argument, several responsibilities

The scientific editor owns the common argument: research object and scope → closest established knowledge → specific unresolved aspect → design that addresses it → evidence → bounded answer. A gap need not be a new algorithm. It can concern applicability, a distinguishable explanation or a previously conflated observable. Identify the predecessor result and its actual limitation; do not infer a literature gap from a convenient set of saved runs.

Give each writer a short shared packet: current central answer, evidence type, scope, object/comparator definitions, decisive source locators, actual results and contrary evidence, adjacent section's outgoing/incoming point, owned paths and return condition. Long context should hold necessary originals and reasoning, rather than the entire conversation. A worker returns complete usable text plus the few unresolved dependencies that affect it.

| Responsibility | Input needed | What the return must establish | Dependency to close |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sources and research lineage | Closest original studies and actual design | Which object/aspect is established, conditional or unresolved; definitions and populations | Editor accepts or narrows the gap before writers use it |
| Methods–results evidence author | Question, equations/procedures, data roles, outputs and adequacy evidence | Every comparison has a defined design; each result uses the same quantities, conditions and observation rules | Introduction's promised route is executable; discussion uses the actual comparisons |
| Introduction writer | Accepted lineage plus evidence-author and discussion returns | Reader can infer why this specific work is needed and why its route addresses the gap | Editor checks promises against Results and Conclusions |
| Discussion and conclusions author | Results, closest factor-specific literature and counterevidence | Evidence changes a named understanding; alternatives and application limits are explicit | Conclusion answers the introductory tension without adding an untested mechanism |
| Title and abstract author | Current complete argument and supported answer | Subject/relationship/scope and distinctive contribution are clear to intended readers | Final cross-check against Introduction, Results and Conclusions |
| Whole-manuscript reviewer | Actual integrated source, figures, definitions and source passages | Reader continuity, scientific support and section responsibilities survive integration | Editor inspects repaired text and closes consequential findings |

Methods and Results usually benefit from a common responsible owner because design, variables, comparison and interpretation depend on one another. This does **not** require combining the printed sections or having one agent conduct all experiments. Split writing when technical domains or volume justify it; then require an explicit shared quantity/comparison map and reciprocal check. The methods author checks that every result follows the executed design; the results author checks that Methods defines every consequential quantity and baseline. Expertise, dependency and cost determine the split, not a rule of one agent per heading.

An Introduction writer can also produce title/abstract later, provided the final input is the whole paper. A working abstract can guide early planning; final title and abstract must reflect the actual evidence and bounded conclusion. Separate a reviewer from the prose it judges where feasible; disclose prior exposure and same-model review. New scientific evidence can reopen the shared argument; optional phrasing changes cannot silently enlarge the contribution.

## Introduction follows the source of evidence

The closing paragraph follows from the specific unresolved aspect: establish the objective, identify the reason for the chosen approach, then outline the main evidence-producing route. Use a transition only when the preceding argument warrants it. It should tell the reader how the question will be answered; the abstract reports the answer. It need not reproduce the abstract's result list or give every setting.

| Evidence type | Background needed to derive the question | Bridge to the present work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experimental | What is observed; which sample/state/condition makes competing explanations or measurement ambiguity consequential; what previous experiments resolved | Explain why the chosen manipulation and measurements distinguish the unresolved aspect, with relevant controls or sampling logic |
| Numerical/modeling | What predictions are needed; successes and limits of the closest models; assumptions, calibration/observation choices or regimes that leave a specific prediction uncertain | Explain what model change or comparison isolates that uncertainty and what evidence assesses its consequence; avoid a catalogue of solvers |
| Theoretical | Known result and assumptions; the specific regime, mathematical property or implication those assumptions leave open | State the new assumptions/derivation and the consequence to be established; numerical examples do not substitute for a proof |
| Mixed | Why one evidence source alone cannot answer the stated problem; what each source constrains | Explain the complementary roles and their common quantities/populations; do not equate a fitted observation with independent validation |
| Review/synthesis | Established competing interpretations and the scope of evidence that can adjudicate them | State the synthesis basis and the comparison or organizing insight, rather than invent an experimental gap |

These are prioritization questions, not templates or paragraph counts. Several tensions may be present, but each must contribute to the main question. Admit prior success before defining its remaining limit. A paragraph can close with a supported recognition or scope condition; it need not announce a new gap.

## Headings, openings and the reverse outline

For major revision, extract the full section/subsection list and one controlling statement per substantive paragraph. Read that outline without the prose. It should expose a research route: problem → design and defined observables → decisive comparisons → explanation → answer. A heading should name a disciplinary object and its relation or role; avoid arbitrary branch names, execution verbs and attractive but undefined concepts. Confirm unusual combinations in the closest originals. Generic conventional headings remain useful when their contents have clear responsibilities.

A section opening before its first subsection is useful when readers need the local purpose, a prerequisite from the previous section, or the reason for the next sequence of comparisons. Give that scientific relationship in a short paragraph. Omit it when the heading and first paragraph already supply the relationship, the venue requires another form, or it merely repeats a list of subsections. Observe actual comparable articles before claiming a venue convention.

Methods prose connects need → design choice → definition → what the definition makes assessable. Results connects comparison purpose → observation and magnitude → evidence-supported inference → next consequential comparison. Provide sources for borrowed choices and locally derived identities where needed. Avoid an execution diary, unexplained symbols, repetitive disclaimers or transitions that imply causality from association.

## Acceptance and efficient return

Before integration the editor checks that the Introduction's promise, the design's comparison, the Results' answer and the Conclusions' implication refer to the same object and scope. Return a short dependency note only where a relation was changed or remains unresolved; do not make every sentence a bureaucratic record. The reviewer supplies the reverse outline and located counterexamples, not just a score.

Two rounds that only exchange synonyms call for reassessing the object, source or argument. Ask a delayed agent whether it is checking sources, reasoning or repeatedly polishing before interrupting it. Continue necessary thinking/source work; request the best complete candidate and unresolved questions when repeated editing is the cause. Further revision follows another agent's concrete feedback.
