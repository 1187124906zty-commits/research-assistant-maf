# Discussion and Conclusions

Interpret the principal findings or design argument in relation to the original question. Explain what they mean, how they compare with prior knowledge and which alternatives remain. A discussion adds reasoning rather than repeating result captions. Do not upgrade association to causality or a model-consistent explanation to unique mechanism.

Decide whether that reasoning belongs beside each result or in a separate synthesis. Use combined Results and Discussion when evidence and local interpretation should be read together; retain a separate Discussion for substantial cross-result explanation, literature reconciliation, implications or common limits. Do not defer all meaning to a distant section merely to preserve a template. An owner of a combined section selects both existing `methods_results` and `discussion_conclusions` scopes and reciprocally checks definitions, comparisons and evidence with the Methods owner; see [chapter contracts](../scientific-writing/references/chapter-contracts.md).

Apply [the compact results/discussion/conclusion criteria](../scientific-writing/references/section-specific-guidance.md#results-discussion-conclusions) for interpretive work, local limits and negative/inconclusive outcomes. MAF directly supplies this portion for this scope; other hosts should read it.

Connect an explanatory assertion to the named result, comparison and source that support its specificity. In integrated evidence units, state why the analysis is needed, report the consequential observation and develop its supported meaning near the evidence. In separate Discussion, retain precise result references rather than unanchored mechanism prose. Address contradictory observations and material uncertainty. Explain how limitations affect particular conclusions, not a generic limitations list. Distinguish investigated boundaries from proposed future tests and transfer beyond the tested domain.

Preserve a necessary connective relationship when moving between quantities, evidence types or explanations; the reader must be able to recover why the next inference follows. Use the actual prerequisite evidence and its status, not a generic validation label. If venue learning is assigned, return located author requirements, comparable interpretive conventions and their justified application to the coordinator; source teaching layouts are not compulsory chapter structures.

For unevaluated methods or systems, discuss design rationale, expected use, failure conditions and evaluation needs explicitly; effectiveness remains a hypothesis. Implications should follow from the supported argument and intended readership, not a grand claim of universal importance.

Conclude with the bounded answer and contribution, retaining the condition essential to interpreting them. Avoid introducing a new unsupported result, reciting the full abstract or ending with generic praise. Future work should address a consequential uncertainty rather than an arbitrary list of extensions.

Read [source-use.md](../scientific-writing/references/source-use.md) for explanatory citations and [argument-and-readers.md](../scientific-writing/references/argument-and-readers.md) for contribution. Return the assigned prose, surviving alternatives and any bounded question that would materially change the interpretation.
