# Introduction

Keep the closing study paragraph focused on how the design answers the established question. Detailed observation algorithms, provenance populations and calibration disclosures belong where the reader needs them to assess the study; retain consequential scope without turning the introduction's ending into a repeated methods inventory. Factual preservation does not require copying all original details or citation keys into each revised section.

Give the intended reader enough context to understand why the question matters. Establish relevant knowledge and explain the source-supported unresolved aspect for original research, or the explanatory need for an expository article, then state the question, approach and contribution. A note explaining a standard result need not claim a field-level gap or new derivation. This is an adaptable logic, not a mandatory number of paragraphs.

Apply [the compact introduction criteria](../scientific-writing/references/section-specific-guidance.md#introduction): choose background through the actual evidence-producing paradigm, and explain each necessary new object's role in the narrowing argument. MAF directly supplies this portion for this scope; other hosts should read it.

Choose background by the contribution, article type and actual evidence or argument route using [chapter contracts](../scientific-writing/references/chapter-contracts.md): experimental manipulation/measurement, numerical assumptions/observables, theoretical assumptions/consequences, or their justified combination. For original research, trace the closest predecessor's result and source-supported remaining limit to the present design; for exposition, explain which known relationship or assumption roles the argument clarifies for its readers. Check the last paragraph against the actual Methods/Results or theoretical argument and Conclusions; it states the objective and answering route, while the abstract condenses the actual answer.

Compare methods, assumptions, findings and limitations rather than listing papers in sequence. A gap can be an unresolved mechanism, uncertain applicability, incomplete comparison or a useful untested condition; do not manufacture a lack of algorithm novelty. Distinguish what literature actually shows from your proposed explanation.

Keep the scope precise enough that the approach can answer the stated question. Describe the contribution without anticipating unsupported outcomes. A short roadmap may help a complex paper, but should not repeat the abstract or become a formulaic closing paragraph. Introduce necessary terms before relying on them and avoid broad significance claims detached from the study.

Return actual introduction text and bounded source/evidence gaps; do not silently broaden the research objective.
