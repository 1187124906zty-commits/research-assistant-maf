# Mechanism analysis: fixed-B constitutive contrasts and surviving explanations

## Scope and evidence status

The retained evidence supports a controlled explanation of response **inside the scalar conduction/phase-change model**: changing the high-temperature conductivity continuation chiefly translates the rear thermal tail, while changing the heat-capacity continuation partly offsets that translation. It does not identify the real liquid conductivity, heat capacity, absorptivity or melt-flow mechanism. This manuscript input interprets existing SimAgent results; no simulations or literature searches were performed. Numerical consistency and manuscript interpretation are separate from physical validation.

Sources are the [evidence dossier](../inputs/evidence-dossier.md), especially “Actual model,” “Six unique production runs and numerical adequacy,” “Condition trends and mechanism evidence,” and “Original numerical paper and competing explanations,” and [verified data](../inputs/verified-data.json), especially `physical_model`, `calibration`, `factorial`, `comparison_rows` and `numerical_evidence`. Original artifact locators below are relative to the source root recorded in those inputs; their contents were inspected by the dossier producer rather than newly replayed here.

The moving-frame balance is ∇·(uH − k∇T)=0 with u=(−v,0,0). This enthalpy transport represents stationary material passing through laser coordinates; it is not computed liquid convection. The model assumes isotropic conduction, constant density and latent heat, an equilibrium linear phase fraction between 1290 and 1350 °C, a circular Gaussian source and a locally steady translating field. Production solves omit liquid momentum, evaporation, free-surface response and radiative/convective feedback. All mushy and liquid states exceed both property-table endpoints: 982 °C for k and 1093 °C for cp. Consequently the contrast concerns constitutive continuations throughout the phase-change region, including temperatures below the solidus above each endpoint; it is not a liquid-only property intervention.

The effective source parameter η=0.2890548519203547 was fitted to B's length proxy. Its +0.158 μm fitting residual is not independent corroboration. A/C are nonblind retrospective comparisons with fixed parameters; B width/depth are post-calibration diagnostics. Local propagation of B's expanded uncertainty gives Δη≈0.01665, not a posterior or full prediction uncertainty. The camera-derived experimental length and the model's two-solidus-crossing length use different operators. These limits prevent an absorptivity-identification or predictive-validation interpretation. ## Rear translation and its interpretation

For HL−LL, the rear solidus and liquidus crossings move by −42.487 and −43.500 μm, respectively; the front solidus moves by only +0.726 μm. The rear liquidus-to-solidus span changes by −1.013 μm from 80.264 μm. For LH−LL, the corresponding rear shifts are +6.223 and +6.530 μm, the front solidus shift is +0.055 μm, and the span change is +0.307 μm. HH−LL gives rear shifts of −33.506/−32.600 μm and a span increase of +0.906 μm. The large length changes therefore primarily reflect common translation of rear phase boundaries, rather than substantial enlargement of the centerline mushy span. This decomposition is directly supported by retained crossing coordinates; it is not a measured freezing-front motion. [Source: verified data `factorial.rows` and crossing/span effects.]

At a matched 1320 °C, holding k reduces it from 32.508 to 25.2 W/(m K), approximately 22.48%, while holding cp reduces latent-inclusive effective heat capacity from 5387.793 to 5336.667 J/(kg K), approximately 0.95%. The latent contribution is 4666.667 J/(kg K) in the mushy interval. The small effective-storage contrast at this temperature helps explain why the mushy-region response is more sensitive to k, but it cannot alone explain the complete field: the cp continuation also changes integrated enthalpy and storage outside the mushy interval. The mushy x-face median coordinate-transport/diffusion Pe rises from 4.437 (LL) to 5.773 (HL), while LH gives 4.390. These diagnostics are consistent with reduced modeled diffusion relative to coordinate transport and a longer rear tail. They do not observe liquid velocities. [Source: verified data `factorial.matched_temperature_properties` and `rows`.]

Temperature-selected control-volume budgets change their integration regions between states. Their closure supports energy accounting, not a fixed-volume causal decomposition. In particular, rear outward diffusive power rises from 14.724 W in LL to 14.913 W in HL despite lower matched-temperature k; altered gradients and integration regions matter. A blanket statement that every diffusive flux decreases would contradict the retained budget. Model surface peaks of 2697–3721 °C in B are internal states without independent temperature validation or evaporation/free-surface physics. They do not establish actual liquid temperature, keyholing or evaporation. Interpolated phase spans, particularly subcell front spans, are not resolved physical interface thicknesses.
